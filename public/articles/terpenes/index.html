Terpenes are one of the most widely found classes of organic molecules in all of nature. They give plants their distinct smells and tastes, and play a crucial role in the cannabis experience beyond just THC and CBD .

Overview

Terpenes are organic compounds naturally produced by plants which create unique smells and flavors. Their natural functions include:

  • Attracting pollinators - Drawing bees, butterflies, and other beneficial insects
  • Deterring pests - Repelling herbivores and harmful insects
  • Protection - Guarding against fungus, microbes, and UV rays
  • Communication - Signaling between plants

In the cannabis world, terpenes create unique profiles that change how flower tastes and feels during consumption .

The Entourage Effect

Terpenes don’t just provide flavor—they work synergistically with cannabinoids like THC and CBD in what researchers call the “entourage effect.” This interaction may influence:

  • The intensity and character of the high
  • Therapeutic benefits
  • Duration of effects
  • Overall experience

Terpene Classification

Terpenes are classified by the number of isoprene units they contain:

Class Isoprene Units Carbon Atoms Example
Monoterpenes 2 10 Limonene, Pinene
Sesquiterpenes 3 15 Humulene, Caryophyllene
Diterpenes 4 20 Cafestol
Triterpenes 6 30 Squalene

Preserving Terpenes

Terpenes are volatile compounds that can degrade with:

  • Heat - High temperatures cause evaporation
  • Light - UV exposure breaks down molecules
  • Air - Oxidation degrades terpene profiles
  • Time - Gradual loss over storage

Proper storage in cool, dark, airtight containers helps preserve the terpene profile.

Did You Know

Drug detection dogs are trained to smell terpenes, not THC itself. This is why they can detect cannabis even in sealed containers—the aromatic terpenes are difficult to fully contain.
