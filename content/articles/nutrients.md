**Nutrients** are the essential elements that plants absorb through their roots (and to a lesser extent, their leaves) to fuel growth, flowering, and overall health. Understanding what nutrients a plant needs — and when — is one of the key skills in growing cannabis.



## Macronutrients (N-P-K)

On most nutrient labels or packages, you'll often see 3 numbers, like `10-10-10`.  

These numbers represent the three primary macronutrients used to feed plants: Nitrogen-Phosphorous-Potassium, often referred to as `N-P-K` (using the element's periodic table abbreviation).

| Nutrient | Symbol | Role |
|----------|--------|------|
| Nitrogen | N | Drives vegetative growth — leaves, stems, and overall plant structure |
| Phosphorus | P | Supports root development, energy transfer, and flower production |
| Potassium | K | Regulates water uptake, enzyme activation, and overall plant resilience |

### N-P-K by Growth Stage

The ratio of these elements/nutrients for fertilizer changes based on the growth stage of the plants, as the plant's needs shift.

- **Seedling** — Very little fertilizer needed. Most soils contain enough to sustain a young plant.
- **Vegetative stage** — Higher nitrogen (N) to support rapid leaf and stem growth. A ratio like 3-1-2 is common.
- **Flowering stage** — Lower nitrogen, higher phosphorus (P) and potassium (K) to support bud development. A ratio like 1-3-2 is typical.
- **Late flower / flush** — Many growers reduce or stop feeding in the final 1–2 weeks before harvest, and provide only water.

## Secondary Macronutrients

These are needed in smaller quantities than N-P-K but are still essential:

| Nutrient | Symbol | Role |
|----------|--------|------|
| Calcium | Ca | Cell wall structure, root growth |
| Magnesium | Mg | Central atom in chlorophyll; essential for photosynthesis |
| Sulfur | S | Protein synthesis, enzyme function |

Calcium and magnesium deficiencies are common enough that many growers supplement with a dedicated **Cal-Mag** product, especially in coco coir or reverse-osmosis water setups.

## Micronutrients

Required in trace amounts, but deficiencies can still cause problems:

- Iron (Fe)
- Manganese (Mn)
- Zinc (Zn)
- Boron (B)
- Copper (Cu)
- Molybdenum (Mo)
- Chlorine (Cl)

Most complete fertilizer lines include these. Deficiencies in micronutrients are less common and usually point to a pH problem rather than a true lack of the element.

## pH and Nutrient Availability

Nutrients are only available to the plant within a certain pH range. Even if the nutrients are present in the soil or solution, the plant cannot absorb them if the pH is wrong. This is called **nutrient lockout**.

| Medium | Ideal pH Range |
|--------|---------------|
| Soil | 6.0–7.0 |
| Coco coir | 5.5–6.5 |
| Hydroponics | 5.5–6.5 |

A pH meter or pH test drops and pH-up/pH-down solutions are considered essential tools for any grower.

## Organic vs. Synthetic

There are two broad approaches to feeding cannabis:

### Organic

Organic nutrients come from natural sources — compost, worm castings, bone meal, bat guano, kelp, and similar amendments. Rather than feeding the plant directly, organic growing focuses on building a healthy soil ecosystem where microorganisms break down organic matter into plant-available nutrients. You may also see this referred to as "living soil".

- Slower release, harder to overfeed
- Builds soil life over time
- Some consider it to produce better flavor
- Less precise control over exact nutrient ratios

### Synthetic (Mineral)

Synthetic nutrients are manufactured salts dissolved in water. They provide nutrients in a form the plant can absorb immediately.

- Fast-acting and precisely measurable
- Easier to correct deficiencies quickly
- Can build up salt deposits in the medium over time
- Requires more attention to runoff and flushing

Some growers use a hybrid approach, combining amended soil with occasional liquid feeds.


## Deficiency and Toxicity

When a plant is not getting the right amount of a nutrient, it shows visible symptoms:

### Common Deficiency Signs

| Nutrient | Symptoms |
|----------|----------|
| Nitrogen | Lower leaves turn pale yellow, then die off from the bottom up |
| Phosphorus | Dark green or purple-tinted leaves; slow growth; poor flowering |
| Potassium | Brown, crispy edges on older leaves (leaf margin burn) |
| Calcium | Brown spots on newer growth; curling leaves |
| Magnesium | Yellowing between the veins of older leaves (interveinal chlorosis) |
| Iron | Yellowing of new growth while veins remain green |

### Toxicity (Overfeeding)

Too much nutrient is just as harmful as too little. Common signs of overfeeding include:

- **Nutrient burn** — Brown, crispy tips on leaves, starting at the edges
- **Dark green, clawed leaves** — Often a sign of nitrogen excess
- **Salt buildup** — White crust on the surface of the soil or around drainage holes

The fix is usually to flush the medium with plain, pH-balanced water and then resume feeding at a lower dose.

## Tips for Beginners

- **Start low** — Use half the manufacturer's recommended dose and increase gradually.
- **Watch the plant, not the calendar** — Feed based on what the plant looks like, not on a rigid schedule.
- **Check pH every time** — Most problems that look like deficiencies are actually pH issues.
- **Less is more** — It is easier to fix an underfed plant than an overfed one.
