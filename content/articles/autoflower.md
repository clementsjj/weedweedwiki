**Autoflowering cannabis** refers to varieties that transition from vegetative growth to flowering based on age, rather than a change in {{% link "photoperiod" %}} (light cycle). This trait originates from {{% link "Cannabis Ruderalis" cannabis-ruderalis %}}, a species native to regions with short summers and long daylight hours.


{{< toc >}}

## Origins

Autoflowering genetics trace back to {{% link "Cannabis Ruderalis" cannabis-ruderalis %}}, which evolved in northern latitudes (Central Asia, Russia, Eastern Europe) where summers are brief. To survive and reproduce in these conditions, ruderalis adapted to flower automatically after a certain age rather than waiting for shorter days.

Modern autoflowers are hybrids, created by crossing ruderalis with {{% link "Cannabis Sativa" cannabis-sativa %}} or {{% link "Cannabis Indica" cannabis-indica %}} varieties. This preserves the autoflowering trait while introducing the potency, flavor, and yield characteristics of sativa and indica genetics.

## How They Work

Traditional {{% link "photoperiod" %}} cannabis requires a shift to 12 hours of darkness to trigger flowering. Autoflowers ignore the light schedule entirely. Once the plant reaches a certain age (typically 2–4 weeks from germination), it begins flowering regardless of how many hours of light it receives.

This is controlled by genetics rather than environment. The plant's internal clock determines when flowering begins.

## Growing Autoflowers

### Advantages

- **Speed** — Most autoflowers complete their full cycle in 8–12 weeks from seed to harvest
- **Simplicity** — No need to change light schedules or worry about light leaks
- **Size** — Compact plants are well-suited for small spaces, balconies, and stealth grows
- **Multiple harvests** — Outdoors, growers can fit multiple harvests into a single season
- **Continuous light** — Can run 18/6 or 20/4 light schedules throughout the entire grow

### Considerations

- **No cloning** — Clones inherit the mother's age, so they flower immediately at a small size. Autoflowers are typically grown from seed
- **Training window** — The short vegetative period means less time to recover from high-stress training techniques
- **Yield** — Individual plants generally produce less than a well-grown photoperiod plant, though this gap has narrowed significantly with modern genetics
- **No veg control** — The grower cannot extend the vegetative phase to grow a larger plant

### Light Schedules

Since autoflowers do not depend on light cycles, growers commonly use:

- **18/6** — 18 hours light, 6 hours dark (most common, balances growth and energy cost)
- **20/4** — 20 hours light, 4 hours dark (more light for faster growth)
- **24/0** — Continuous light (debated; some growers believe a dark period is still beneficial)

## Modern Autoflowers

Early autoflower hybrids in the 2000s were often criticized for low potency and small yields. Through years of selective breeding, modern autoflowers have improved dramatically. Many now rival photoperiod strains in {{% link "THC" %}} content, terpene profiles, and yield relative to their size.
