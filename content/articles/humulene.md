**Humulene** is a monocyclic sesquiterpene found abundantly in hops (*Humulus lupulus*) and in varying quantities in [cannabis](cannabis). It is responsible for the distinctive earthy, woody aroma associated with beer and contributes to the complex scent profile of many cannabis strains.


## Aroma Profile

Humulene produces a distinctive combination of scents:

- Earthy
- Woody
- Spicy
- Herbal
- Subtle hop-like bitterness

## Natural Sources

Humulene is found in many plants beyond cannabis and hops:

- **Hops** - Primary source, gives beer its characteristic aroma
- **Cannabis** - Present in many strains
- **Black pepper** - Contributes to its spicy notes
- **Sage** - Part of its herbal scent
- **Ginseng** - Traditional medicinal herb
- **Clove** - Aromatic spice

## Potential Benefits

Research suggests humulene may have several therapeutic properties:

- **Anti-inflammatory** - Studies show potential for reducing inflammation
- **Appetite suppressant** - Unlike many cannabinoids, may reduce appetite
- **Antibacterial** - Shown activity against certain bacteria
- **Antitumor** - Early research indicates possible anticancer properties

## The Entourage Effect

Humulene often works synergistically with other [terpenes](terpens) and [cannabinoids](cannabinoids) in what is known as the "entourage effect." When combined with [CBD](cbd) or other compounds, its therapeutic potential may be enhanced.

## Humulene in Cannabis Strains

Strains high in humulene typically have earthy, woody flavor profiles. Some strains known for elevated humulene content include:

- Girl Scout Cookies
- White Widow
- Headband
- Pink Kush
- Sour Diesel

## Relation to Hops

Humulene gets its name from *Humulus lupulus*, the scientific name for hops. Cannabis and hops are botanical relatives, both belonging to the family [Cannabaceae](cannabinaceae). This shared ancestry explains why beer and certain cannabis strains can have similar aromatic qualities.
