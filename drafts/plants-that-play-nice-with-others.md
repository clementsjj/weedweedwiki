Plants that grow well with others while returning nutrients to the soil are primarily nitrogen-fixers (legumes) and dynamic accumulators (deep-rooted plants that bring minerals to the surface). These plants act as natural fertilizers, improving soil health and supporting neighboring crops. 
Here are the best plants for these purposes:
1. Nitrogen-Fixing Legumes (Add Nitrogen) 
Legumes form a symbiotic relationship with bacteria in the soil to convert atmospheric nitrogen into usable fertilizer. 

    Peas (Garden and Snow): Excellent in spring or fall, they provide nitrogen to the soil that benefits future crops. The entire plant can be chopped and left on the surface as green manure.
    Beans (Bush, Pole, and Fava): A staple for soil improvement, particularly in the "Three Sisters" method (corn, beans, squash). They provide nitrogen to nitrogen-hungry plants like corn and tomatoes.
    Clover (White or Red): An excellent living mulch for pathways or under fruit trees, clover fixes nitrogen and creates a self-sustaining nutrient cycle, reducing the need for fertilizer.
    Lupine: A hardy perennial or annual with beautiful flowers that fix nitrogen, improving poor soil for surrounding plants, such as red columbine.
    Hairy Vetch: A robust nitrogen-fixing winter cover crop that enriches soil with both nitrogen and phosphorus. 

2. Dynamic Accumulators and Soil Improvers (Add Minerals) 
These plants use deep taproots to pull nutrients like potassium, calcium, and magnesium from deep within the soil, storing them in their leaves. 

    Comfrey: Famous as a "mineral miner," its deep taproots mine nutrients, which are then released into the soil when its leaves are used as mulch or compost.
    Borage: A friendly neighbor to almost everything, it is a pollinator magnet that brings potassium and calcium into the soil when it decomposes.
    Dandelion: Although considered a weed, its deep taproots break up compacted soil and bring nutrients up to the surface.
    Sunflowers: They have deep taproots that improve drainage and, when composted, add significant organic matter. 

3. Alliums and Herbaceous Helpers (Pest Repellents) 
These plants improve soil structure and prevent diseases while growing beside others. 

    Garlic & Chives: These repel pests like aphids and root nematodes, and their decomposing leaves enhance soil fertility.
    Marigolds: They contain compounds (thiophene) that repel soil-damaging root nematodes and are excellent when chopped and buried in the soil at the end of the season.
    Basil: Grows exceptionally well with tomatoes, contributing to soil health and repelling pests. 

Best "Soil-Returning" Combinations

    Three Sisters: Corn (provides structure), Beans (fix nitrogen), Squash (shades soil to retain moisture).
    Tomato + Basil + Marigold: Basil provides a symbiotic root relationship, and marigolds repel nematodes.
    Carrots + Lettuce + Onions: Their roots grow to different depths, reducing competition while improving overall soil structure. 

Pro Tip: To get the best nutrient return, leave the roots of legumes in the ground after harvest, and chop the upper plant material to decompose on the soil surface
